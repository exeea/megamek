# Exterior Battle Armor and swarming

The GPU board presents exterior passengers and hostile swarmers as separate units whose individual figures ride on the carrier's posed model. Both cameras, picking, shadows and effects use these same instances. Selection and commands still use the existing game entities.

`GpuBoardSource` captures exterior occupancy from `Entity.getExternalUnits()` and the carrier's swarm-attacker ID. Internal bay passengers stay hidden. Identified exterior Battle Armor can be captured even when transport has cleared its own board position. Both the passenger and carrier must pass the existing visibility checks. The immutable `BoardScene.Attachment` belongs to the Swing snapshot; no renderer reads live entities.

`UnitAttachments` places suits against the existing arm, leg, torso and hull joints. Suit identities keep stable slots when another suit is lost. Each suit retains its own scale and follows carrier movement, torso twist, jumping, falling and hover. Hostile suits hold with one arm and repeatedly strike with the other. Boarding and normal dismounts blend into alternating climbing motions; forced releases flail and tumble before settling.

Friendly riders and a hostile swarming squad can share a carrier. Their attachment surfaces are assigned by role, independently of which other squads are visible or attached. Friendly grips stay on the sides and rear. Swarmers use the fronts of Mek limbs and torso, or the front and rear of a vehicle hull. Quadrupeds use their front legs in place of arm grips. Spacing accounts for the suits' actual width, including on narrow central torsos. A squad joining, leaving or becoming concealed cannot move the other squad's grips. A brush-off only releases the hostile squad when the game confirms that result.

Attachment changes run in `UnitPlayback`, with the same pause, speed, instant and concealment handling as movement and attacks. Separately delivered passenger packets cannot release a swarmer before the carrier's path arrives. Jump arrival flows directly into release. A water release partway along a received route splits playback at the recorded drop hex, then resumes the remaining route.

Brush-off and deliberate shake-off attempts publish confirmed `ResolvedAttack` events, including failures. Failed attempts animate the carrier while retaining its attachment. A successful brush releases at contact. The renderer never rolls for success or changes transport/swarm relationships. Stop-swarm events identify voluntary dismounts; stale movement flags are not used to infer a later jump dislodgement.

Release paths start from the displayed attachment pose and finish at the captured destination's normal formation. The path bows away from the hull, samples the rendered ground for clearance, and eases into its exact endpoint. Figures removed by the result remain visible through their departure. Water entry produces a separate splash and foam ring for each figure, using the existing water effect batch and playback clock. Destroyed figures sink before disappearing.

Water does not automatically detach every Battle Armor unit. In the current rules, `drownSwarmer` specifically handles conventional infantry. These visuals animate actual relationship changes and preserve Battle Armor attachment whenever the game preserves it.

The attachment locations are derived from the shipped rigid rigs and their mesh bounds. They are general placements, not individually authored hand contacts for every chassis. Unusual custom hulls or arm layouts may need art tuning. The arc clearance is a presentation aid, not a rigid-body collision simulation against every prop or building. No platform or performance claims are made here.

Verification is covered by `UnitAttachmentsTest` (visibility, simultaneous friendly/hostile occupancy, transport distinction, timing, pause/instant/concealment and endpoints), `GpuAttachmentSourceTest` (real Swing captures, null-position riders, unloading, passenger/path packet order and water-route splitting), and `GpuAttachmentSmokeTest` (native biped/quad/vehicle poses, boarding, release continuity and failed brush-off attachment). The native review checks six friendly riders and six swarmers together, including all rider/swarmer pairs throughout the strike loop, spacing within the hostile squad, stable positions when the other squad appears, and friendly riders retaining their positions during hostile boarding and release. It writes exterior model and fall review images to the Gradle build directory's `gpu-board-review` folder; `exterior-shared-*-full.png` shows friendly riders in blue and hostile swarmers in red.

On 2026-09-27, the shared-carrier follow-up passed 29 focused tests across the attachment, source and playback suites, including consecutive releases and concealment during a deferred shake-off attempt. The native attachment review also passed, including water-entry spray and foam and an OpenGL error check. Earlier verification found two existing volley-checkpoint failures both with this change and with the original `UnitPlayback` compiled separately: `GpuScenePlaybackTest.eachAttackAppliesDestructionAndVisibilityAtImpactBeforeRecoveryOrTheNextAttack` and `GpuSceneSourceTest.batchedAttacksCaptureBoardChangesBetweenResultsBeforeAnotherSwingRefresh`. No changes were made to those tests or to volley grouping.
