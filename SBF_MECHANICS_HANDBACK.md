## Implementation log

This initial ledger precedes implementation. Subsequent entries distinguish verified live behavior from remaining rules and interfaces. A successful compile or a game opening is insufficient evidence for a full ruleset. No changes have been pushed.

### Phase sequence and reconnect

IO:BF “Playing the Game”, pp. 163–164, and “Detection and Reconnaissance Phase”, p. 195. The optional phase is “after the Initiative Phase”. Each phase completion now makes one transition. Reconnaissance no longer re-enters deployment or starts multiple movement phases, and a targeting report waits for its normal acknowledgement. A reconnect sends the current turn (including the last one) only to its recipient and cannot consume a turn or phase. Pending report packets retain their contents when the next phase clears its work list. Regression coverage: SBFPhaseEndManagerTest and SBFGameManagerPendingPacketTest.

### Ground initiative and turn order

IO:BF “Initiative Phase”, p. 163, and “Unequal Number of Formations” and “Combat Phase”, p. 164. Unequal sides are “moved in proportion”. SBFInitiativeHelper now uses the Team initiative already rolled and reported by the server. Allied owners contribute to the same side’s movement count when team initiative is enabled. The losing side declares all combat actions first. SBFInitiativeHelperTest reverses the individual and side rolls and checks both phase orders. Advanced reconnaissance’s undetected-formation exception remains open.

### Ground terrain and movement modes

IO:BF “Movement Cost Table”, p. 165, and “Terrain Modifiers on Movement”, p. 166, say “treat the entire Formation as having to pay that cost” when one constituent pays extra. SurfaceSBFMoveStep uses SBFTerrainMovement for constituent terrain legality and costs, including infantry in urban terrain, naval/submarine water requirements, sealing and amphibious abilities, tracked and wheeled movement, VTOL, WiGE and rail. SurfaceSBFMoveStepTest covers representative legal and prohibited combinations. Mixed-width water, entry shore, bridge capacity, unusually large Elements and advanced bogging remain PARTIAL. The standard road allowance and stacking are recorded with server movement below.

### Shared combat state and pickup outcomes

“Step 6: Roll for Critical Hits”, p. 171, requires another roll for “further Armor loss”. SBFUnit stores the results, including cumulative targeting, weapon and movement effects or mission kill. BaseFormationConverter supplies the same movement calculation at conversion and after casualties. SBFGame owns pending hits, allocations, engagement orders, and the removal of destroyed or withdrawn Formations. A Formation's original and current movement are distinguished.

“Victory Conditions”, p. 174, says retreated Formations “are counted as destroyed”. SBFVictory evaluates the surviving sides after all End effects; reserves count, observing or single-side setups cannot win by default, and mutual elimination is a draw. Scenario-defined endings use the scenario's winner. SBFVictoryTest covers reserves, withdrawal, simultaneous loss, a lone side, team results and a scripted round limit.

### Standard attack declarations and allocations

“Step 5b: Apply Damage”, p. 170: “Each player rolls 1D6”. SBFCombatDamage elects one allocator for the exchange, gives the defender ties, enforces the two-hit distribution limit, and replays accepted allocations in firing order irrespective of when each owner answers. An unresolved choice keeps the firing report open and survives a save. The simplified alternative on p. 171 is enabled only by `base_sbf_simplified_damage`. A single remaining defending Unit needs no choice. SBFCombatDamageTest and SBFAttackProcessorTest exercise both paths. SBFToHitData is authoritative for target, range, withheld fire and secondary target checks. Current and historic reports share the same saved audience.

### Command, deployment and End Phase

“Force Commander and Formation Leaders”, p. 163: “a single Unit in their Force”. Setup checks one COM per owner and one LEAD per multi-Unit Formation. `/sbfcommand com|lead <formation ID> <unit number>` changes an owner's choices before combat. Existing authored designations and the loader's existing LEAD handling are used. The Unit's parent receives COM. SBFCommandDesignationTest checks cross-Formation reassignment. Deployment and End Phase share the game's turn list, live Formation state, and report history. The End Phase orders combat losses before morale, engagement and victory. SBFDeployCommandTest exercises rejected placements and successful reserve entry.

### Transport, engagement, morale and withdrawal

“Transporting Infantry” and “Mounting and Dismounting”, pp. 167–168, charge the carrier “1 MP” for each pickup and drop. SBFTransport uses the player's route, the carrier's remaining capacity, the carried Formations and shared movement/stacking rules. A dropped Formation can act with its prescribed MP reduction; carrier casualties force dismounts. `/sbftransport` submits mount and drop waypoints. SBFTransportTest covers ownership, capacity, movement cost, external riders, casualty dismount and prohibited terrain. Air drops under p. 209 are separate.

“Engagement Control”, pp. 167–168, and “Ending Engagements”, p. 172. SBFEngagementControl accepts standing or prompted owner decisions. A pending move is held until choices are known; resolution uses the traversed route and records the engaged opponents on the Formations. `/sbfengage` exposes the same decisions from either board. Immediate overrun, evade and forced engagement are being tested.

“Forced Withdrawal”, p. 172, requires movement “toward its home map edge at its best possible speed”. SBFWithdrawal uses the existing pathfinder, designated edge, actual movement and embarked passengers. “Recovering Morale”, p. 174, permits morale to “improve by one level”. SBFMoraleResolver resolves End losses before recovery. Twelve SBFMoraleResolverTest cases passed in the preceding run. Only the documented ground cases are verified.

### Scenario endings and objective conditions

“Alternate Victory Conditions”, p. 174, permits “other scenario-specific victory conditions”. Existing MM scenario predicates now understand live SBF Formations. UnitPositionTrigger checks a deployed Formation against its own board. BattlefieldControlTrigger distinguishes unteamed enemies. KilledUnitsTrigger distinguishes actual losses from retreats when a scenario expressly asks for kills. Pickup elimination continues to count retreats. SBFVictoryTest exercises all three predicates.

“Capturing Objectives”, p. 174, requires “two consecutive End Phases”. SBFScenarioProgress and SBFScenarioResolution use the scenario's ObjectiveMarker objects and a surviving, continuously present Unit. The optional suggested victory-point system and final salvage are selected by `base_sbf_victory_points` and `base_sbf_salvage`. An objective-specific scenario trigger may name a marker and player. Simultaneous claims by hostile sides have no precedence specified in this text. The server reports these for scenario adjudication. Marker locations use the ground board supported by the existing scenario format.

The Clan probe's separate 9-hex row is not recoverable from a generic PRB rating alone. “Visual Identification” and “Recon Scan”, p. 196, specify same-hex identification and “spend 1 MP”. The server performs visual checks and accepts paid scans during `/sbfrecon`; its movement cost is read by the existing pathfinder and validator. A natural 2 identifies the scanner. The same filtered contact representation is sent on reconnect and on live updates. Weak contacts do not send their exact coordinates. Sensor ghost facing and airborne recon require additional state. SBFDetectionHelperTest exercises table boundaries, scan spending, ownership, and weak versus located contacts. Independent review passed these ground scan results and identified additional contact-loss cases on p. 198.

### Reconnaissance, combat and advanced initiative checkpoints

“Visual Identification” and “Aerial Recon”, p. 196, use the existing SBFDetectionHelper with the actual ground route. When a located counter crosses an enemy in the same hex its identification rises to Eyes on Target. Hardened urban cover lasts until Engagement Control or combat at that location under the p. 197 table footnote. Leaving the hex ends the exception. A quiet Squadron in qualifying airspace detects exposed formations. A Squadron entering Central can order one scan per eight path hexes, or one per distinct target with PRB/RCN, using `/sbfrecon air <squadron> <contact>`. Scans use the nearest actual flight-path hex, including through-transit, and resolve after declarations. An enemy attack can cancel the mission. The printed rules do not assign an unambiguous base sensor range to a summary-only fighter with no identified sensor equipment. Such a fighter still performs eligible visual detection. Capital space-based detection remains MISSING.

Opponent rosters and private spotter, target and force hierarchy IDs are filtered on both reconnect and live updates. Independent review caught a hidden ID in a visible allied force and a lost visible subforce under an excluded parent. Forces.visibleTo now projects individual units and parentage. It also caught a legal aerial scan lost when the squadron ended in Inner. Both fixes passed the independent recheck.

“Types of Attacks”, p. 169, and “Underwater Combat”, p. 171, are served by SBFCombatCommand, SBFCombatControl, SBFStandardUnitAttack and SBFToHitData. The server rebuilds orders and uses the same terrain and contact rules for combat and retreat. “Critical Hits”, p. 171, and “Salvage”, p. 174, retain hit cause across random and chosen allocations, including older pending combat data. Ground and atmospheric spotting, interleaved allocation, per-Unit attack limits, source-preserving casualty resolution and filtered secondary target declarations passed independent source review.

“Advanced Initiative”, pp. 194–195, “Battlefield Intelligence”, pp. 206–207, and “Banking Initiative”/“Forcing the Initiative”, p. 223. SBFInitiativeModifiers computes the best modifier in each printed category using living Units and the previous End state. SBFScenarioProgress holds assignments, BI, commander loss, armor crossings, pre-plots and unspent points. SBFInitiativeCommand accepts pre-roll choices. The deployment roll on p. 163 receives no advanced initiative modifiers. Independent review found and the author corrected that setup-roll issue and missing explicit SCOM. Recheck and regression run pending. The p. 207 MHQ-density rule and worked example disagree; the implementation uses the written requirement of one MHQ1+ Unit per Formation.

### Immediate combat, transport and hidden units

“Ambush Attacks From Hidden Formations”, p. 214, gives “the attacker’s choice of Short or Medium” and applies damage immediately. SBFContactDamage uses the same immediate hit allocation, criticals, underwater weapon damage and casualty handling as the other movement attacks. The ambusher loses 2 MP and takes +2 on later attacks that turn. The defender can answer after reconnecting. Mixed support vehicles and mobile structures use their actual Elements when checking concealment. Independent review found a submerged ambusher could initially use Short and full Medium damage. The corrected rejection and half damage passed re-review. The integrated run verified both range choices, declined ambush, owner authorization, and the turn penalties.
